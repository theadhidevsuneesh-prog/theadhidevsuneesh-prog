# Read this first {.u9 data-eyebrow="Welcome"}

## What this book is

Calculus has a reputation for being scary. It isn't. Behind every symbol there is a simple physical idea, and once you *see* the idea, the symbols stop being a wall and become a shorthand. This book is written for someone who has never done calculus: every chapter starts from the picture, builds the idea in plain words, and only then gives the rule, the proof sketch, the exam tricks and worked examples.

It follows your syllabus exactly:

![The three units and how they fit together](figs/fig_big_picture.svg){width=100%}

| Unit | Syllabus topics (as given) | Chapters |
|---|---|---|
| **I** Matrix methods and linear systems | review of matrices, elementary operations, rank, linear dependence and independence of vectors, solution of linear systems, inverse of matrices, eigenvalues and eigenvectors and their properties, Cayley–Hamilton theorem | 1 – 8 |
| **II** Differential calculus and its applications | derivatives of standard functions and general rules, parametric, implicit, logarithmic differentiation, higher-order derivatives, Rolle's theorem, mean value theorems, Taylor's and Maclaurin's theorems, indeterminate forms, L'Hôpital's rule, maxima and minima | 9 – 19 |
| **III** Fundamentals of integral calculus | general rules of integration, substitution, integration by parts, integration by partial fractions, properties of definite integrals | 20 – 26 |

Chapter 0 is a **toolkit** of school-level facts (functions, algebra, trigonometry, logarithms). If any of it is shaky, calculus will feel hard for no good reason, so spend an evening there.

## How to read each chapter

::: {.idea t="The five-step habit"}
1. **Read the picture first.** Every chapter opens with a "Big idea" box and a diagram.
2. **Cover the solution.** For each *Example*, try it yourself before reading the solution.
3. **Learn the formula boxes by heart.** They are the exam currency.
4. **Do the *Try it* boxes.** They take two minutes each and expose gaps immediately.
5. **Finish with the chapter practice.** Answers are in the book; your professor's practice sets (MCQ deck, partial-fraction and definite-integral sheets, textbook exercises) are mapped to chapters in Appendix D.
:::

## The colour code

These boxes appear throughout. Learn to recognise them; they tell you how to read what is inside.

::: {.idea t="Big idea"}
The intuition in one paragraph. If you remember nothing else, remember this.
:::

::: {.def t="Definition"}
A precise statement of what a word means. Exams quote these almost verbatim.
:::

::: {.thm t="Theorem"}
A fact that has been proved. Usually comes with conditions that you must check before using it.
:::

::: {.formula t="Formula"}
Worth memorising.
:::

::: {.warn t="Watch out"}
A trap that costs marks. Every one of these has caught real students.
:::

::: {.tip t="Exam tip"}
A shortcut, a pattern the examiners love, or a way to check your answer.
:::

::: {.try t="Try it"}
A quick question. Answer in the margin before reading on.
:::

::: {.story t="Story time"}
A little history or a real-world picture, to make the idea stick.
:::

## A suggested 4-week plan

| Week | Focus | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chapter 0, Unit I (Chapters 1–8) | Be able to row-reduce a matrix without thinking; solve any 3-variable system; find eigenvalues |
| 2 | Chapters 9–14 (limits → higher derivatives → Rolle/MVT) | Differentiate anything in the table; implicit, log and parametric differentiation on autopilot |
| 3 | Chapters 15–19 (Taylor, L'Hôpital, applications, optimisation) | Handle any max/min problem with a clear 5-step method |
| 4 | Unit III (Chapters 20–26) + all appendices | Integrate by substitution, parts and partial fractions; use property tricks for definite integrals |

::: {.tip t="How much maths per day?"}
Ninety focused minutes with a pen beats four hours of re-reading. Maths is learnt with the hand, not the eye.
:::
